About the lab

Climate, water and the communities of the Himalaya

HIMPACT Lab works on how climate affects water resources in the Himalaya. The lab is based in the School of Civil and Environmental Engineering (SCENE) at IIT Mandi, Himachal Pradesh. Our work runs from atmospheric drivers of extreme rainfall to streamflow, snowmelt and flood response in mountain basins such as the Beas.

Recent work includes a hydrometeorological reconstruction of the July 2023 Himachal Pradesh floods. It used ERA5 wind profiles, snowmelt fields and remote-sensing flood extents for Kullu and Mandi. Other work covers heatwave anomalies over northwest India and decade-by-decade temperature projections for the state.

Research objectives

  1. Develop reliable atmospheric, hydrological and hydrodynamic model parameterizations for accurate simulation of hydroclimatic fluxes.
  2. Build a hydrologic decision-support system that couples process-based modelling with deep learning to predict and manage extreme events.
  3. Assess long-term climate-change impacts on Himalayan communities and design adaptation strategies suited to local conditions.
Research themes

What we work on

Our work spans the chain from the atmosphere to the river. Throughout, we focus on extremes and on information that people can act on.

Climate change impact assessment

CMIP6-based projections of precipitation and temperature extremes and their consequences for Himalayan water resources.

Droughts & climate extremes

Drought propagation, teleconnections such as ENSO, compound extremes and the role of elevation and catchment storage.

Early warning & climate information

Early-warning and climate-information systems that turn forecasts into timely, usable guidance.

Hydrological modelling

Numerical and data-driven approaches, including SWAT, for flood and drought prediction in data-scarce mountain basins.

Coupled atmosphere–hydrology

Linking atmospheric and hydrological models to trace extreme rainfall through to runoff and flooding.

AI & deep learning for water

Machine-learning and deep-learning models for peak-flow estimation, time-series forecasting and remote-sensing analysis.
